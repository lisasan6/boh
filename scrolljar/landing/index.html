How it works · 1

A real block

Pick your apps and your hours. iOS enforces the shield itself — not a reminder you can swipe away. We never even learn which apps you chose.

How it works · 2

A breath before you choose

Opening anyway is always allowed. First comes one slow breath. Then staying is one easy tap, while going on takes a deliberate one. In a peer-reviewed study, a short pause before opening led people to back out of 36% of their attempts to open an app.

Grüning, Riedel & Lorenz-Spreen, PNAS (2023)
How it works · 3

A slip becomes a gift

Opening anyway costs the price you set. Why money? Because a price is friction you can feel: just enough to make you stop and ask whether going further is worth it. If it is, the coin drops in the jar and the app opens for the window you chose. No lecture, no judgement, just a little good done.

The science

Built on evidence, not vibes.

The core of ScrollJar is built on peer-reviewed research. Here are the four studies that shaped it most, and what each one became in the app.

31%

It isn't just you

A major study estimated that self-control problems drive about a third of time on social apps. Given the choice, most people set binding limits for themselves.

In ScrollJar: deals you make in cold blood, for your future self.

Allcott, Gentzkow & Song, “Digital Addiction,” American Economic Review (2022)
36%

A pause changes behaviour

With a brief pause before opening an app, people backed out of 36% of attempts. In a follow-up experiment, an easy way out did the most, and a lecture did nothing.

In ScrollJar: one slow breath before you open, with staying the easy choice.

Grüning, Riedel & Lorenz-Spreen, PNAS 120(8) (2023)
13pp

Your own money works, if you choose it

Among people willing to put their own money on the line, it beat a cash reward by about 13 percentage points. Most people weren't willing, so making it optional matters.

In ScrollJar: “Make it real” is optional, at a price you set yourself.

Halpern et al., New England Journal of Medicine 372 (2015)
RCT

Blocking actually helps

Two weeks of blocked mobile internet measurably improved attention, mental health and wellbeing in a randomised trial, even though most people found the block hard to keep.

In ScrollJar: a real block, drawn by iOS itself, not a reminder.

Castelo et al., PNAS Nexus 4(2) (2025)

Questions

The honest answers.

Does it really block apps, or just remind me?

It really blocks. ScrollJar is built on Apple's Screen Time framework — the same system-level engine behind parental controls — so the shield is drawn and enforced by iOS itself, above everything else on screen.

What if I genuinely need to get in?

You always can. Opening a blocked app costs the price you set in advance and opens for a window you chose. There's no lockout, no permission to ask for, and no way for us to say no.

Is ScrollJar against social media?

No. There's nothing wrong with entertainment, and the creators you love aren't the problem. Autopilot is: the open you didn't decide on, the feed that quietly takes an hour. ScrollJar puts a pause right there, so what you watch is what you chose. Think of picking a film on demand instead of watching whatever happens to be on TV.

Why does opening cost money?

Because a price is friction you can feel. A tap is easy to brush past; a coin you priced yourself makes you stop and ask whether going further is worth it right now. Sometimes it is, and that's fine: the coin goes 100% to your charity, so even a yes does some good. You set the price, so it can be high enough to make you think and never high enough to hurt.

Where does the money actually go?

All of it goes to the charity you pick, settled monthly through a nonprofit donation platform, with receipts you can check. ScrollJar keeps none of it. Card payments carry a small processing fee: on the free plan it's shown and added on top, so the full price you set still arrives. Members never see it, because we cover it.

Then how do you make money?

Memberships, and nothing else. No ads, no data sales, no share of your slips. It matters that we never profit when you struggle — that's the conflict of interest we were built to avoid.

Can you see what I do on my phone?

No. Apple hands apps like ours anonymous tokens instead of app names, so we never learn which apps you blocked, when you use them, or what you do inside. We couldn't spy on you if we wanted to.

Is there an Android version?

iPhone first. Android has no official blocking API, and recent releases restrict the workarounds every blocker relies on. We'd rather ship one honest version than two weak ones — Android comes when we can do it properly.

We're building it now, iPhone first. Join the waitlist and you'll hear from us exactly once — the day it's ready.
