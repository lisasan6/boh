The science

Built on evidence, not vibes.

Every mechanism in ScrollJar exists because a peer-reviewed study said it works. Here are the four that shaped it.

31%

It isn't just you

Self-control problems drive roughly a third of social media use, and people will gladly pay for tools that help their future selves.

Allcott, Gentzkow & Song, “Digital Addiction,” American Economic Review (2022)
36%

Friction alone changes behaviour

A brief pause before opening an app led people to abandon 36% of attempts. The way out did the work; lectures did nothing.

Grüning, Riedel & Lorenz-Spreen, PNAS 120(8) (2023)
13pp

Your own money beats a reward

Putting your own money at risk beat being paid the same amount to succeed. Loss moves people; bonuses don't.

Halpern et al., New England Journal of Medicine 372 (2015)
RCT

Blocking actually helps

Two weeks of blocked mobile internet measurably improved attention, mental health and wellbeing in a randomised trial.

Castelo et al., PNAS Nexus 4(2) (2025)
Why nowIn 2026 a Los Angeles jury found platforms liable for addictive design (now on appeal); 20+ countries are acting. Court record & press reports

We also read the studies that disagree with us. No screen-time app has been through a peer-reviewed trial yet — including ours. We intend to be the first.

Questions

The honest answers.

Does it really block apps, or just remind me?

It really blocks. ScrollJar is built on Apple's Screen Time framework — the same system-level engine behind parental controls — so the shield is drawn and enforced by iOS itself, above everything else on screen.

What if I genuinely need to get in?

You always can. Opening a blocked app costs the price you set in advance and opens for a window you chose. There's no lockout, no permission to ask for, and no way for us to say no.

Where does the money actually go?

All of it goes to the charity you pick, settled monthly through a nonprofit donation platform, with receipts you can check. ScrollJar keeps none of it. Card payments carry a small processing fee: on the free plan it's shown and added on top, so the full price you set still arrives. Members never see it, because we cover it.

Then how do you make money?

Memberships, and nothing else. No ads, no data sales, no share of your slips. It matters that we never profit when you struggle — that's the conflict of interest we were built to avoid.

Can you see what I do on my phone?

No. Apple hands apps like ours anonymous tokens instead of app names, so we never learn which apps you blocked, when you use them, or what you do inside. We couldn't spy on you if we wanted to.

Is there an Android version?

iPhone first. Android has no official blocking API, and recent releases restrict the workarounds every blocker relies on. We'd rather ship one honest version than two weak ones — Android comes when we can do it properly.

When does it launch?

We're building it now, iPhone first. Join the waitlist and you'll hear from us exactly once — the day it's ready.
