# ScrollJar — Psychology Playbook

**Design rules with receipts.** Compiled July 27, 2026, from three verified research sweeps (habit & behavior change · motivation & social · prosocial emotion). Every rule below is mapped to an app surface and cited. Companion docs: science-dossier-bibliography.md, evidence-dossier.md. Replication status is flagged honestly — the brand is "science-based," so the science must be bulletproof.

---

## 1 · Onboarding & commitment creation

- **Everything is chosen, nothing imposed.** Self-determination theory: controlling external constraints undermine motivation; autonomy sustains it (Ryan & Deci 2000). The user picks apps, schedule, price, charity — ScrollJar only ever enforces the user's own choices. This is why a self-set penalty preserves dignity where a parental-controls vibe wouldn't.
- **Offer smart defaults.** People voluntarily self-impose binding constraints and they work — but self-set ones are calibrated suboptimally (Ariely & Wertenbroch 2002, Psych Science; replicated 2024). Suggest evidence-based defaults (€5, 15-min windows) rather than a blank slider.
- **Elicit an if-then plan.** Implementation intentions: d = 0.65 across 94 studies (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006). Onboarding asks: *"If I reach for Instagram at night, then I will ___."* WOOP-style setup (wish → outcome → obstacle → plan) is defensible but modest (metas d ≈ 0.28–0.34) — say "helps," never "transforms."
- **Specific beats vague.** Goal-setting theory (Locke & Latham 2002): concrete targets ("under 45 min/day") outperform "use my phone less."
- **Anchor to values, not restriction.** The intention question ("what is this hour for?") carries the meaning payload — acting on chosen values improves well-being (ACT literature; Steger's meaning research). The promised payoff is a richer life, not a smaller phone bill.
- **Time the invitation.** Fresh start effect: commitment spikes at temporal landmarks — Mondays, month starts, birthdays, new phones (Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014; field-replicated in savings enrollment 2021). Also: habits are most changeable within ~3 months of life disruptions — new job, move, breakup (Verplanken & Roy 2016). Marketing calendar and prompt timing should ride landmarks.
- **Identity framing — use lightly.** "Be a reader" vs "read more" plausibly helps, but the famous "be a voter" result failed field replications (Gerber et al. 2016). Fine as flavor, never as a claimed mechanism.

## 2 · The shield & the breath pause

- **The pause works; the lecture doesn't.** The one sec PNAS decomposition (Grüning et al. 2023): friction cut actual app openings ~57% over six weeks — and the *easy dismiss option* was the most effective component; the deliberation *message* alone did nothing. Design consequence: the breath pause needs a prominent, warm "never mind, close" path — and no moralizing text during the pause.
- **Urge surfing decouples urge from action.** Brief mindfulness instruction reduced smoking without reducing urge frequency (Bowen & Marlatt 2009); mindful attention lowers cue-induced craving (Westbrook et al. 2013). The pause isn't there to kill the urge — it's there to let the urge pass through without steering.
- **Treat the reach as boredom-intolerance.** People prefer electric shocks to sitting with their thoughts (Wilson et al., Science 2014); boredom proneness predicts problematic phone use (Elhai et al. 2018). A gentle reframe at the shield ("this itch is where ideas come from") turns the enemy moment into the product's kindest touch.

## 3 · The override & the coin

- **Frame the donation as repair, not purchase.** "Conscience accounting" (Gneezy, Imas & Madarász, Mgmt Science 2014): after a transgression people prefer to donate *immediately* and it genuinely relieves guilt — transgress→donate ordering works as moral *cleansing/repair*, closing the loop. Copy direction: "made it right," never "unlocked."
- **But never let the fine become a price.** Gneezy & Rustichini's daycare study: a small fine can become a fair fee that licenses the behavior. Before payment: broken-promise framing. After payment: consolation. Plus the price-comfort watchdog (cheerful repeat overrides → suggest raising the price).
- **Licensing risk is real but modest — and mostly public.** Moral licensing meta d ≈ 0.31 with publication-bias concerns (Blanken 2015); a 2025 meta finds licensing mainly when behavior is *observed*. ScrollJar's private-first override design already weakens the licensing pathway.
- **Loss aversion: ~2:1, honestly stated.** Best current meta: λ ≈ 1.95, CI [1.82, 2.10] (Brown et al., J Econ Literature 2024); weaker at small stakes. A modest real fee leverages it; say "about twice as heavy," never "always twice."
- **Keep agency inside the penalty.** Warm glow: voluntary giving activates reward circuitry more than mandatory (Harbaugh et al., Science 2007, small N) — the user chose the charity and the amount in cold blood; remind them of that authorship at the coin moment.

## 4 · The rough day (the scientifically strongest differentiator)

- **The shame spiral is the relapse engine.** Abstinence violation effect (Marlatt): attributing a lapse to internal, stable causes ("I'm weak") plus shame predicts full relapse; confirmed in real-time EMA studies. The post-override and end-of-bad-day copy must externalize ("a hard evening," "the scroll won this round") and immediately prompt a small next plan.
- **Guilt repairs; shame destroys.** Tangney's well-replicated distinction: guilt ("I did a bad thing") motivates repair; shame ("I am bad") predicts hiding and re-offense. Every string targets the behavior, never the person. This is the psychological law under the Voice Law.
- **Self-compassion fuels the retry.** Self-compassion after failure increases self-improvement motivation (Breines & Chen 2012), correlates with less procrastination and better health behavior (Sirois 2015 meta), and intervention RCTs show moderate effects (Ferrari 2019, 27 RCTs). It is not complacency — that's the misconception to design against.
- **Offer a fresh start only after rough patches.** Resets help strugglers but *demotivate* people doing well (Dai 2018, "double-edged sword"). Restart prompts appear after expensive weeks — never as a global mechanic.

## 5 · Streaks & progress

- **Never-resetting streaks are directly supported.** Streaks become goals in themselves and a broken chain reduces subsequent engagement; de-emphasizing the break or offering repair mitigates it (Silverman & Barasch, JCR 2023). ScrollJar's lifetime clean days + "coin day" calendar is the evidence-aligned design.
- **Honest habit timeline.** Median ~66 days to automaticity, range 18–254; missing a single day makes no material difference (Lally et al. 2010). Frame progress in weeks-to-months and say so when a day is missed.
- **Show milestone proximity.** Goal-gradient: effort accelerates near a goal, even with head-start credit (Kivetz et al. 2006). Small wins drive inner work life (Amabile) — surface "3 fewer opens than yesterday."

## 6 · Stats & tracking

- **Measure the enemy, not the joy.** Etkin (JCR 2016): quantifying enjoyable activities boosts quantity but drains enjoyment and intrinsic motivation. ScrollJar quantifies scrolling avoided and money donated; what people *did* with reclaimed time stays narrative and unmeasured (photos, one-line stories — the mural).
- **The year-in-review is an identity engine.** Wrapped-style retrospectives function as celebratory self-narrative users adopt for identity display (Annabell & Rasmussen 2025). "Your Year in the Jar" — hours returned, meals funded — is the deep-stats add-on's crown feature and the best organic-share surface.

## 7 · The social layer

- **Kudos are evidence-backed.** Kudos received predicts subsequent activity on Strava (Social Networks 2022); exercise is causally contagious among 1.1M runners, strongest between comparable peers, flowing from slightly-behind to slightly-ahead (Aral & Nicolaides, Nature Comms 2017). Finite feed + kudos is the right social spine; match comparable peers in challenges.
- **Celebrate outcomes, never announced intentions.** Gollwitzer 2009 ("When Intentions Go Public"): announcing identity-relevant intentions that get acknowledged produces premature completeness and *reduces* striving. The feed shows weeks survived and euros donated — never "Luca committed to quitting TikTok!"
- **Support, never control.** Social support helps; monitoring/nagging "control" raises distress and hiding (Lewis & Rook 1999). No policing mechanics, no "call out a friend," no reciprocity-pressure nudges ("you haven't kudos'd back").
- **Norms need approval framing.** Descriptive norms boomerang — below-average users regress toward the mean; an injunctive signal (the smiley) eliminates it (Schultz et al. 2007). Community stats read "92% of jars survived this week 💛," never raw failure counts.
- **No leaderboards.** Competitive leaderboards lowered intrinsic motivation and outcomes in the canonical semester-long study (Hanus & Fox 2015); gamification metas show modest, fragile effects at best. Comparison target = your own past self.

## 8 · Charity & impact display

- **One vivid unit beats statistics.** Identifiable victim effect (Small, Loewenstein & Slovic 2007): a single concrete beneficiary outperforms numbers — and adding statistics *reduces* giving. "€5 ≈ 10 school meals," one image, no data dumps. Unit asking (Hsee 2013) supports the same shape.
- **Say "100% reaches the program" — explicitly.** Overhead aversion: donations rise sharply when overhead is covered (Gneezy, Keenan & Gneezy, Science 2014). ScrollJar's we-keep-nothing model is precisely the highest-converting frame; print it at every donation touchpoint.
- **Giving feels good — under conditions ScrollJar controls.** Prosocial spending → happiness is real but smaller than the 2008 headline (registered replications mixed; robust in large samples per Aknin/Dunn 2022). Amplifiers: visible impact, autonomous choice, connection to the cause — all three are design decisions ScrollJar already makes. Never oversell "donating makes you happy."

## 9 · The weekly recap (a sacred touchpoint)

- **One concrete prompt, modest expectations.** "Three Good Things" improves well-being modestly (Seligman 2005; later RCTs confirm smaller-than-first-claimed effects). ScrollJar's version: *"Name one thing you did with a returned hour."* Specific and behavioral; feeds the mural (opt-in).

## 10 · The jar as character

- **Care sustains behavior — probably.** Tamagotchi effect and virtual-pet health studies are suggestive, not RCT-grade; Finch's success is press-level evidence. Direction: the jar *benefits* from your success (smiles, fills, thrives) and never suffers or guilt-trips when you slip — borrowing the care motive without shame leverage. A/B test rather than bet the product on it.

## ⛔ Never say / never claim (the honesty shield)

1. **"Dopamine detox"** — pseudoscience; dopamine doesn't deplete or reset (Harvard Health). Never use "dopamine" in marketing at all; the Good Dopamine *design* principle is internal vocabulary.
2. **"Willpower is a muscle" / ego depletion** — failed multi-lab replications (d ≈ 0.04). ScrollJar's honest line: we engineer friction so you don't need willpower.
3. **"21 days to form a habit"** — myth (real range 18–254 days).
4. **The "65%/95% accountability" statistic** — fabricated marketing lore with no primary study. Never quote it. Cite Giné/Karlan/Zinman's RCT instead.
5. **StickK's "72% success"** — platform-reported, selection-biased. Use RCT-grade numbers.
6. **"Losses hurt twice as much"** as a universal law — say "about twice, on average."
7. **Overselling giving-makes-you-happy** — real, small, conditional.
8. **Noun-magic identity claims** ("saying 'I'm a reader' changes your brain") — contested replications.

## The one-paragraph scientific pitch (press-ready)

ScrollJar's loop is assembled from replicated findings: friction at the moment of opening cuts app use by half (PNAS 2023); people knowingly pay for commitment devices because ~31% of their social media use is self-control failure (AER 2022); self-set financial stakes leverage ~2:1 loss aversion (J Econ Lit 2024) and raised quit rates in the classic deposit-contract RCT (AEJ 2010); donations immediately after a slip convert guilt into repair (Mgmt Science 2014); and shame-free, self-compassionate failure handling is the strongest known buffer against the relapse spiral (Marlatt; Tangney; Breines & Chen). No other app in the category is built on all five.
