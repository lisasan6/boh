## Part 1 — The legal record: courts are calling it addictive

### The landmark verdict — *K.G.M. v. Meta & YouTube* (March 25, 2026)
First-ever jury verdict holding social platforms liable for addiction-related harm. Bellwether in JCCP 5255, Los Angeles Superior Court (Judge Carolyn B. Kuhl). Plaintiff ~20, started YouTube at 6, Instagram at 11 (NPR); compulsive use → anxiety, depression, body dysmorphia, suicidal ideation via infinite scroll, filters, algorithmic amplification. Zuckerberg gave his first-ever jury testimony (Feb 18, 2026). Snap and TikTok settled before/at trial start (confidential).

**The jury found:** Meta (Instagram) and Google (YouTube) **negligent** in designing/operating their platforms; negligence a "substantial factor" in the harm; **failure to warn**; both acted with **"malice, oppression or fraud."** Fault split Meta 70% / Google 30%. $3M compensatory + $3M punitive ($6M total). Post-trial motions denied (June 2026); **Meta filed a notice of appeal in early July and YouTube on 13 July 2026 (AP). No appellate ruling yet as of Oct 2026.** *(The "malice, oppression or fraud" and "failure to warn" findings were not re-verified.)* The design-feature theory (infinite scroll, notifications, autoplay, recommenders) sidestepped Section 230 by targeting product architecture, not content. Commentators called it Big Tech's **"big tobacco moment."**
Sources: [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/3/26/jury-finds-meta-youtube-liable-for-social-media-addiction-what-we-know) · [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/03/25/nx-s1-5746125/meta-youtube-social-media-trial-verdict) · [CalMatters](https://calmatters.org/economy/technology/2026/03/youtube-facebook-loss-in-social-media-addiction-trial/) · [Clyde & Co analysis](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/april/navigating-the-first-instance-judgments-in-social) · [MDL update](https://mdlupdate.com/mdl/3047-social-media-adolescent-addiction/)

### The wave behind it
- **Federal MDL 3047** (N.D. Cal.): ~2,900 cases by July 2026; 10,000+ individual claims, ~800+ school-district suits, 40+ state AG actions. Design-defect and failure-to-warn claims survived Section 230. First school-district bellwether (Breathitt County, KY) **settled before trial on 21 May 2026** (Meta last, after TikTok, Snap and YouTube earlier that week). Terms weren't disclosed in court; settlement agreements Reuters obtained through a public-records request show **~$27M total** (Meta $9M, Snap $8M, ByteDance $8M, Alphabet $2.01M). The companies deny the allegations. Next school-district bellwethers: Tucson & Charleston, Feb 2027. [AP via WCPO](https://www.wcpo.com/news/state/state-kentucky/meta-settles-social-media-addiction-case-brought-by-rural-kentucky-school-district) · [Reuters via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/meta-paid-9-million-settle-234825076.html) · [Engadget](https://engadget.com/2184229/meta-tiktok-snap-youtube-settle-kentucky-school-social-media-addiction-lawsuit/)
- **State AGs vs Meta: settled, approved by the court.** Trial opened 18 Aug 2026 in Oakland. On **26 Aug 2026** Meta and the states filed a consent judgment that Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers approved the same day. Meta pays **$12.19B over 10 years, rising to $17.1B** if TikTok, YouTube and Snapchat agree to comparable terms (headlines vary: "up to $17B" from California, "about $18B" from CNN including other states). For under-18s the default terms are: a **2-hour daily limit** and a **midnight–6am block** (both liftable only by a parent), no notifications 10pm–7am or in school hours, no like counts, no cosmetic-surgery filters, an optional non-personalized feed, age assurance, and an **independent auditor**. 51 attorneys general joined. **Meta denied the allegations and admitted no wrongdoing.** [California AG](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-secures-transformative-17-billion-settlement-meta) · [Connecticut AG](https://portal.ct.gov/ag/press-releases/2026-press-releases/settlement-with-meta) · [NPR via WUNC](https://www.wunc.org/2026-08-26/california-says-states-meta-agree-to-17-billion-settlement-in-child-safety-trial) · [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/26/tech/meta-states-settle-trial-children)
- **New Mexico:** on **24 March 2026** (the day before K.G.M.) a Santa Fe jury found Meta liable under the state's Unfair Practices Act for misleading the public about platform safety; the evidence centred on child sexual exploitation. It imposed the $5,000-per-breach maximum, totalling **$375M**. This was a **child-safety deception case, not an "addictive design" verdict.** On **6 Aug 2026** the judge also ordered Meta to pay **$567M** into a five-year youth-harm abatement fund (public-nuisance claim). Meta says it will appeal. [KFOX14/CBS4](https://abcnews4.com/news/nation-world/jury-orders-meta-to-pay-375-million-in-new-mexico-child-safety-deception-case) · [The 74 / Source NM](https://www.the74million.org/article/new-mexico-judge-orders-meta-to-pay-567m-for-youth-harm-abatement-fund/)
- **EU (DSA):** Commission **preliminarily found TikTok's "addictive design" breaches the DSA** (Feb 6, 2026 — infinite scroll, autoplay, push notifications; the Commission's own release says these "shift the brain of users into 'autopilot mode'") and issued **parallel preliminary findings against Meta** (July 10, 2026; Meta disagrees and points to Teen Accounts). **Both still preliminary as of Oct 2026; no fine or final decision.** TikTok already forced to permanently withdraw its Lite Rewards programme EU-wide (2024). Fines up to 6% of global turnover possible. [EC — TikTok](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-preliminarily-finds-tiktoks-addictive-design-breach-digital-services-act) · [EC — TikTok Lite](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/tiktok-commits-permanently-withdraw-tiktok-lite-rewards-programme-eu-comply-digital-services-act) · [Slaughter & May — Meta](https://thelens.slaughterandmay.com/post/102nbs3/meta-joins-tiktok-in-the-commissions-crosshairs-over-addictive-design-under-th)
- **Florida:** on **14 Oct 2026** the 11th Circuit reversed a preliminary injunction against HB 3 (no accounts under 14 on covered platforms; parental consent for 14–15), finding the law likely constitutional. The court described it as a "conditional ban" on "platforms' use of addictive features and algorithms". The case returns to the district court; Florida had been enforcing the law since a Nov 2025 stay. [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/11th-circuit-sides-with-florida-over-social-media-ban-for-minors/)
- **Brazil:** the **ECA Digital (Law 15.211/2025) is in force from 17 March 2026**. It requires platforms to limit features that artificially extend use, naming autoplay, rewards for time spent and notifications, and to set defaults that avoid "uso compulsivo"; under-16s' accounts must be linked to a parent. [Planalto (law text)](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2023-2026/2025/lei/L15211.htm). *(Not re-verified: the consumer institute's ~$525M suit vs Meta, TikTok & Kwai, and the 2025 Supreme Court liability ruling.)* [TechResearch](https://techresearchonline.com/news/brazil-sues-social-media-platforms/)
- **France:** Paris prosecutors opened a **criminal investigation into TikTok's algorithm** (4 Nov 2025) over the risk of pushing young people towards suicide content; TikTok "strongly refute[s]" the accusations. [AFP via The Local](https://www.thelocal.fr/20251105/tiktok-denounces-french-criminal-probe-over-youth-suicides)

### Their own documents (alleged in court filings)
- **"Project Mercury"** (Meta + Nielsen, 2020). These are **allegations in a plaintiffs' brief** filed by school districts in MDL 3047 with redactions removed (reported by Reuters on 22 Nov 2025). The underlying documents were **not public**, so do not call them "unsealed". The brief alleges that users who deactivated Facebook for a week reported **lower depression, anxiety, loneliness and social comparison**, and that Meta halted the study. An employee is quoted comparing it to the tobacco industry "doing research and knowing cigs were bad and then keeping that info to themselves." **Meta says the study was stopped because its methodology was flawed.** Always present this as alleged, with Meta's response. [Reuters via Insurance Journal](https://amp.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2025/11/24/848854.htm) · [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/social-media/meta-allegedly-buried-research-showing-its-products-are-harming-users-152236073.html)

**Honest counterpoint (know it before a journalist raises it):** enforcement is leaky. **eSafety's own evaluation (31 Jul 2026)** found 81.5% of Australian under-16s still used a restricted platform three months in (85.9% before); account holding fell only from 52.4% to 42.1%. Courts push back too: France's ban was struck down, and US judges have blocked laws such as Virginia's on First Amendment grounds (though Florida's survived on appeal). That's precisely ScrollJar's opening: top-down bans can't reach adults and struggle even with kids; a *self-chosen* commitment device is the piece of the dismantling that no court can strike down.

## Part 3 — The science (headline findings)

Full annotated bibliography with ~25 papers: **science-dossier-bibliography.md** in the project. The spine of it:

1. **Self-control failure is measured, not assumed.** Allcott, Gentzkow & Song (*AER* 2022, "Digital Addiction"): their economic model estimates that self-control problems account for ~31% of social media use, and people knowingly **pay for commitment tools** to lock themselves out. ScrollJar's premise is this paper's headline result.
2. **Users are trapped, not choosing.** Bursztyn et al. (*AER* 2025, "When Product Markets Become Collective Traps"): many active users **would prefer TikTok/Instagram not exist** — they use it because everyone else does. ScrollJar is an individual exit from a collective trap.
3. **Leaving improves lives — causally.** Allcott et al. (*AER* 2020): deactivating Facebook for 4 weeks increased well-being and durably reduced use. Braghieri, Levy & Makarin (*AER* 2022): Facebook's college rollout caused depression +7%, anxiety +20%. Castelo et al. (*PNAS Nexus* 2025): blocking all mobile internet for two weeks (calls and texts still worked) improved sustained attention, mental health and well-being; 91% improved on at least one.
4. **Friction works.** Grüning et al. (*PNAS* 2023, the one sec study): a simple friction screen led users to abort **36% of app-opening attempts**; attempts fell ~37% over six weeks.
5. **Money stakes work.** Giné, Karlan & Zinman (2010): smoking deposit contracts raised quit rates ~30%+. Halpern et al. (*NEJM* 2015): among people willing to put their own money down, deposits beat equal-sized rewards by ~13 percentage points. **Caveat:** few people chose deposits (13.7% accepted vs 90% for rewards), so overall the reward programs did better (15.7% vs 10.2% quit). Always keep the "among those willing" qualifier.
6. **The design warnings.** Gneezy & Rustichini ("A Fine Is a Price"): a small fine can become a fair price — ScrollJar's penalties must stay framed as broken commitments, never purchases. Breines & Chen (2012): self-compassion after failure beats shame for behavior change — the rough-day response is evidence-based.
7. **The slot machine is real.** Schultz et al. (*Science* 1997) on dopamine prediction error; Schüll's *Addiction by Design*; reportedly a Meta scientist: "intermittent rewards are most effective (think slot machines)" *(not re-verified; don't quote publicly until sourced)*. ScrollJar's fixed, earned rewards are the deliberate opposite.
8. **The gap ScrollJar can own:** no stakes-based screen-time app has any published evaluation. The first RCT is up for grabs — one sec's PNAS paper shows the playbook and the credibility payoff.
