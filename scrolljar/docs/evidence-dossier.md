## Part 1 — The legal record: courts are calling it addictive

### The landmark verdict — *K.G.M. v. Meta & YouTube* (March 25, 2026)
First-ever jury verdict holding social platforms liable for addiction-related harm. Bellwether in JCCP 5255, Los Angeles Superior Court (Judge Carolyn B. Kuhl). Plaintiff ~20, started YouTube at 6, Instagram at ~9; compulsive use → anxiety, depression, body dysmorphia, suicidal ideation via infinite scroll, filters, algorithmic amplification. Zuckerberg gave his first-ever jury testimony (Feb 18, 2026). Snap and TikTok settled before/at trial start (confidential).

**The jury found:** Meta (Instagram) and Google (YouTube) **negligent** in designing/operating their platforms; negligence a "substantial factor" in the harm; **failure to warn**; both acted with **"malice, oppression or fraud."** Fault split Meta 70% / Google 30%. $3M compensatory + $3M punitive. New-trial motions denied; both appealing (July 2026). The design-feature theory (infinite scroll, notifications, autoplay, recommenders) sidestepped Section 230 by targeting product architecture, not content. Commentators called it Big Tech's **"big tobacco moment."**
Sources: [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/3/26/jury-finds-meta-youtube-liable-for-social-media-addiction-what-we-know) · [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/03/25/nx-s1-5746125/meta-youtube-social-media-trial-verdict) · [CalMatters](https://calmatters.org/economy/technology/2026/03/youtube-facebook-loss-in-social-media-addiction-trial/) · [Clyde & Co analysis](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/april/navigating-the-first-instance-judgments-in-social) · [MDL update](https://mdlupdate.com/mdl/3047-social-media-adolescent-addiction/)

## Part 3 — The science (headline findings)

Full annotated bibliography with ~25 papers: **science-dossier-bibliography.md** in the project. The spine of it:

1. **Self-control failure is measured, not assumed.** Allcott, Gentzkow & Song (*AER* 2022, "Digital Addiction"): ~31% of social media use is driven by self-control problems, and people knowingly **pay for commitment tools** to lock themselves out. ScrollJar's premise is this paper's headline result.
2. **Users are trapped, not choosing.** Bursztyn et al. (*AER* 2025, "When Product Markets Become Collective Traps"): many active users **would prefer TikTok/Instagram not exist** — they use it because everyone else does. ScrollJar is an individual exit from a collective trap.
3. **Leaving improves lives — causally.** Allcott et al. (*AER* 2020): deactivating Facebook for 4 weeks increased well-being and durably reduced use. Braghieri, Levy & Makarin (*AER* 2022): Facebook's college rollout caused depression +7%, anxiety +20%. Castelo et al. (*PNAS Nexus* 2025): two weeks without mobile internet improved attention, mental health, well-being.
4. **Friction works.** Grüning et al. (*PNAS* 2023, the one sec study): a simple friction screen led users to abort **36% of app-opening attempts**; attempts fell ~37% over six weeks.
5. **Money stakes work.** Giné, Karlan & Zinman (2010): smoking deposit contracts raised quit rates ~30%+. Halpern et al. (*NEJM* 2015): losing your own money beats equivalent rewards by ~13pp.
6. **The design warnings.** Gneezy & Rustichini ("A Fine Is a Price"): a small fine can become a fair price — ScrollJar's penalties must stay framed as broken commitments, never purchases. Breines & Chen (2012): self-compassion after failure beats shame for behavior change — the rough-day response is evidence-based.
7. **The slot machine is real.** Schultz et al. (*Science* 1997) on dopamine prediction error; Schüll's *Addiction by Design*; Meta's own scientist: "intermittent rewards are most effective (think slot machines)." ScrollJar's fixed, earned rewards are the deliberate opposite.
8. **The gap ScrollJar can own:** no stakes-based screen-time app has any published evaluation. The first RCT is up for grabs — one sec's PNAS paper shows the playbook and the credibility payoff.
