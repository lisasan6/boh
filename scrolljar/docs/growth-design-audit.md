# ScrollJar × Growth.Design — Psychology Audit

*Their 100+ principle taxonomy (Information / Meaning / Time / Memory) applied to what we've actually built. July 28, 2026.*

Growth.Design is a catalogue of the psychological levers products use. That makes it two things for us: a checklist of what we're leaving on the table, and — just as importantly — **a list of the levers we're refusing to pull**, which is our whole brand.

---

## 1. What we're already doing right (their names for our decisions)

**Self-Initiated Triggers** — "users are more likely to interact with prompts they set up for themselves." This is the entire product. The shield quotes the user's own words back ("You said this hour was for your novel"), which is the strongest possible version of this principle.

**Commitment & Consistency** — the seal ritual, the cold-blood rule, the 24-hour loosening delay.

**Loss Aversion** — the self-set coin, at honest ~2:1 strength.

**Spark Effect** (small effort first) + **Default Bias** — the 2-minute first block, Ghost Jar with no card, coins private by default, quiet posts as the default.

**Feedforward** — the override screen states exactly what will happen before you act: the price, the charity, the window.

**Noble Edge Effect** — "users prefer socially responsible companies." We're not decorating with it; the giving is structural. Onboarding card three says it in six words.

**Delighters** + **Sensory Appeal** — the Nintendo layer, the minted coin, the breathing jar.

**Picture Superiority** — photo posts in the feed beat any stat we could print.

**Reciprocity** — invites that donate $1.

**Aesthetic-Usability Effect** — the whole Aurora Chrome pass. Users read beautiful products as easier ones.

**Fresh Start Effect** — restart prompts after rough patches only (and never for people doing well — that's the documented boundary condition).

## 2. The eight highest-leverage gaps

**① Provide Exit Points — the most on-brand principle in their entire library.** They mean "invite users to leave at the right moment." Every other app treats this as a cost; for us it's the thesis. Right now our screens just… end. Design real exits: after a weekly recap, "That's everything. Go be somewhere else." After a completed focus session, the app should actively close itself. **This deserves to be a signature, not a detail.**

**② Peak-End Rule.** Judgement of an experience = its peak + its ending. Our peak exists (the monthly tip-out ceremony). Our *endings* are unmanaged — sessions end in a toast. Every meaningful moment needs a designed last beat: the last thing you see after a resisted temptation, after a session, after a rough day.

**③ The Aha! Moment is mislocated.** We treat "apps are blocked" as activation. The real aha is **the first temptation resisted** — the instant someone feels the product work. Today that's a small toast. It should be the biggest, warmest moment in the first week: a full-screen beat that names what just happened ("That's the whole product. You just didn't scroll.").

**④ Singularity Effect** — "users care disproportionately about an individual versus a group." Our impact copy says "≈166 meals," which is a statistic, and statistics measurably *reduce* giving. Each tip-out should carry **one** concrete story or beneficiary, with the aggregate available underneath.

**⑤ Goal Gradient Effect** — motivation rises near a goal. We show streaks but never proximity. "Three nights to your 15" is free motivation, and it's honest.

**⑥ IKEA Effect + Endowment** — people value what they build and what feels theirs. We're partway (naming app tokens, writing intentions). Push further: let people **name their jar**, choose its charity ritual, pick the coin's cause per commitment. The more of it they author, the less they'll abandon.

**⑦ Labor Illusion** — "people value things more when they see the work behind them." Our donation flow is invisible: coins go in, a number appears. Show the machinery — the monthly settlement processing, the charity receipt arriving, the ledger being tallied. Trust comes from visible work.

**⑧ Zeigarnik Effect, used honestly.** Incomplete things are remembered. Not "your streak is at risk," but a gentle unfinished-intention thread: "Chapter three is still waiting." The user's own goal, not our engagement metric.

## 3. The levers we refuse — and why that's the brand

Growth.Design documents these neutrally. We reject them explicitly, and saying so publicly is a differentiator no competitor can copy without contradicting themselves.

**Variable Reward.** Their catalogue's most powerful engagement lever, and the exact mechanism of the slot machine we exist to fight. Already in our parking lot, permanently.

**Scarcity / urgency.** No countdown discounts, no "limited spots." A commitment device that manufactures panic is a contradiction.

**Sunk Cost Effect.** We could trap people with "look how much you've invested." We won't — leaving honestly is always free, with 24 hours' notice.

**Bandwagon + Social Proof pressure.** We use social *support*, never social pressure. No follower counts, no leaderboards, no "12 friends are ahead of you."

**Hawthorne Effect.** Behaviour changes under observation — which is precisely why coins are private by default and the boss can never see individual data in Teams.

**Cashless Effect — inverted, and this one's a gift.** Their principle: "people spend more when they can't see the money." Every fintech uses this to make spending frictionless. **We do the exact opposite**: the coin is minted, it drops with weight, it stacks visibly, the jar fills, the receipt prints. We make money maximally *visible* so that spending it is a real decision. That inversion belongs in the manifesto.

**Decoy Effect.** Three tiers could easily be built as a decoy structure. Ours must be honest: Legend exists because half of it is given away, not to make Hero look clever.

## 4. What I'd change in the prototype next

1. **First-resist celebration** — turn the aha moment into a designed full-screen beat (currently a toast).
2. **Exit points** — a proper closing beat on the weekly recap and after focus sessions: the app telling you to go.
3. **One-story impact** — replace "≈166 meals" with a single named story per tip-out; keep the total below it.
4. **Goal proximity** — "3 nights to 15" on the home streak chip.
5. **Name your jar** — one field, big ownership payoff.
6. **Visible settlement** — a small "how your money travels" sequence on the tip-out, showing the work.
7. **Peak-end pass on every overlay** — check the *last* frame of each flow, not just the first.
8. **A public "levers we refuse" page** — the section above, published. It's marketing, recruiting, and a promise all at once.
