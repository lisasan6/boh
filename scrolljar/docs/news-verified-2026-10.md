## 1. Summary: what changed since July

The picture is stronger than in July, but several July entries were wrong or out of date. **The state attorneys general case against Meta ended in a settlement the federal judge approved the same day (26 Aug 2026):** $12.19B over ten years, rising to $17.1B if TikTok, YouTube and Snapchat agree to similar terms, plus default limits for under-18s (two hours a day, a midnight–6am block, no notifications at night or in school hours) and an independent auditor; Meta admitted no wrongdoing. New Mexico added a **$567M court-ordered abatement fund** (6 Aug) on top of its $375M jury verdict, and Meta says it will appeal both. **Both Meta and YouTube appealed the K.G.M. verdict in July.** The Breathitt County school case **settled on 21 May (not June)** for about $27M in total, a figure that came from settlement agreements Reuters obtained, not from the court. **France's under-15 ban was struck down** by the Conseil constitutionnel on 14 Aug, so it never took effect. **Florida** won at the 11th Circuit on 14 Oct (case continues). **Australia passed a law raising the maximum penalty** (11 Sep), and eSafety's own evaluation found 81.5% of under-16s still using social media after three months. The EU findings against TikTok and Meta are **still preliminary**; no fine or final decision was found. July's "Project Mercury" entry overstated things: the documents were **not unsealed**; the claims come from a plaintiffs' brief, and Meta disputes them.

**Corrections to `evidence-dossier.md`:** K.G.M. started Instagram at 11, not ~9 (NPR). The Australian "4.7M accounts" were "deactivated, removed or restricted", a preliminary figure. The ">85% still using" counterpoint is from eSafety, not Newcastle University. Australia's penalty was A$49.5M until the September 2026 law (not A$99M "in June"; June was only the announcement). France was never "in force". The Breathitt settlement was in May. And on Halpern 2015, see section 5.

### 2026-08-26 · Meta settles with state attorneys general; court approves same day
In the multistate case (trial opened 18 Aug in Oakland, N.D. Cal.), Meta and the states filed a consent judgment that Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers approved hours later. The fixed payment is $12.19B over 10 years, rising to $17.1B if TikTok, YouTube and Snapchat agree to comparable terms. (Headline figures vary: California says "up to $17 billion"; CNN says "about $18 billion" including other states' claims.) Product terms for under-18s: a default two-hour daily limit, a midnight–6am block (both liftable only by a parent), no notifications 10pm–7am or during school hours, age assurance, an optional non-personalized feed, and an independent auditor. 51 attorneys general joined. **Meta denied the allegations and admitted no wrongdoing.**
**Status:** Settled (consent judgment approved by the court)
**Sources:** [California AG](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-secures-transformative-17-billion-settlement-meta) (primary) · [Connecticut AG](https://portal.ct.gov/ag/press-releases/2026-press-releases/settlement-with-meta) (primary) · [Proposed consent judgment, Case 4:23-cv-05448-YGR, Doc. 572-1, filed 08/26/26](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/settlements-agreements/california-et-al-v-meta-platforms-inc-et-al-settlement-2026.pdf) (primary) · [NPR via WUNC](https://www.wunc.org/2026-08-26/california-says-states-meta-agree-to-17-billion-settlement-in-child-safety-trial) · [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/26/tech/meta-states-settle-trial-children) · [Hunton analysis](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/court-approves-meta-settlement-with-29-states-over-alleged-harms-to-children-and-teens)

### 2026-08-14 · France's top constitutional body strikes down the under-15 ban
Decision n° 2026-911 DC declared Article 1 (the ban on social networks for under-15s) unconstitutional. It found the ban too broad (no assessment of each service's risks, no parental option) and said it forced everyone, adults included, to prove their age without legal safeguards. The Council acknowledged that the legislature aimed to protect children from risks "notamment d'addiction". It ruled only on Article 1, so the rest of the law (e.g. the phone ban in lycées) was not examined. LCP reports the government intends to rewrite the text.
**Status:** Struck down
**Sources:** [Conseil constitutionnel, Décision n° 2026-911 DC](https://www.conseil-constitutionnel.fr/decision/2026/2026911DC.htm) (primary) · [LCP (Assemblée nationale channel)](https://lcp.fr/actualites/reseaux-sociaux-pourquoi-le-conseil-constitutionnel-a-censure-l-interdiction-pour-les)
**Quotes:** "L'article 1er de la loi visant à protéger les mineurs des risques auxquels les expose l'utilisation des réseaux sociaux est contraire à la Constitution." · "les dispositions contestées portent à cette liberté une atteinte qui n'est pas adaptée, nécessaire et proportionnée" · "toute personne, même majeure, fasse la preuve de son âge" · "Rendu public le 14 août 2026." LCP: "Le gouvernement entend désormais réécrire le texte."

## 4. Counterpoints (verified)

- **Bans are leaky (eSafety's own data, 31 Jul 2026).** Three months in, 81.5% of under-16s still used a restricted platform (85.9% before). Account holding fell from 52.4% to 42.1%. eSafety blames platforms' weak age assurance. Al Jazeera: "more than eight in 10". Sources: [eSafety release via Medianet](https://newshub.medianet.com.au/2026/07/early-insights-from-esafetys-comprehensive-evaluation-project/164211/) (primary) · [Al Jazeera, 3 Aug 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/3/australias-under-16-social-media-ban-failing-study-shows-what-it-means). Quotes: "the proportion of under-16s holding a social media account in Australia fell from 52.4% to 42.1%" · "was 85.9%. This declined to 81.5% at the 3-month follow-up" · "most under-16s who had social media accounts before commencement were able to either retain them or create new ones".
- **US courts block some state laws on First Amendment grounds.** In late Feb 2026 (reported 1 Mar) a federal judge preliminarily blocked Virginia's SB 854, which would have limited under-16s to one hour a day, while recognising the state's interest in "the addictive aspects of social media". Source: [PYMNTS, citing Reuters](https://www.pymnts.com/cpi-posts/federal-judge-halts-virginias-social-media-restrictions-for-minors/). Quote: "The court recognizes the Commonwealth's compelling interest in protecting its youth from the harms associated with the addictive aspects of social media … However, it cannot infringe on First Amendment rights". *Florida (above) went the other way, so the US picture is mixed.*
- **France:** the constitutional ruling above shows blanket bans can fail on free-expression and privacy grounds.
- **Platforms deny it, every time.** Meta denied the states' allegations and admitted nothing in the settlement. It says teen mental health "cannot be linked to a single app" and disagrees with the EU findings. Google says YouTube is "not a social media site". TikTok "strongly refute[s]" the French probe. Breathitt defendants "denied the allegations". (Sources in the timeline entries.)
- **Status reminders for copy:** K.G.M. is on appeal; New Mexico will be appealed; the EU findings are preliminary; the Florida ruling is preliminary-stage; Project Mercury is an allegation.
